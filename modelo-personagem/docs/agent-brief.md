# Brief: rebuilding parts of the "Alpha" (Punishing: Gray Raven) 3D model

The user asked for a 3D model of Alpha (PGR) as close as possible to the
reference, and said the current one "looks like modelling clay (massinha)".
Your job is to rebuild ONE part of it so it reads like a crisp anime game
model: hard edges where the design has them, correct shapes and details
from the official references, clean toon shading. Work calmly and verify
with renders; quality over speed.

## Project

Root: `modelo-personagem/` (code in `src/`).
Everything is generated by Python. Pure-numpy builders produce meshes
(`MeshData`) that are cached in `cache/<part>.npz`; `scene.py` (run inside
Blender 4.5) loads the cached parts, applies modifiers/materials and renders.

* Python with numpy/scipy/skimage/PIL (use it for building):
  `BP=/opt/blender-dl/blender-4.5.14-linux-x64/4.5/python/bin/python3.11`
* Build a part: `cd src && $BP build_parts.py <part>` (parts are listed in
  `parts.py`; yours is named in your task).
* Render: `cd src && ./preview.sh <OUTDIR> <views> [--parts a,b,c] [--res 0.7] [--cam x,y,z,tx,ty,tz,span]`
  - views: `ref` (same camera as the reference image, 735x1030),
    `front`, `three` (3/4), `side`, `back`, `upper`, `face`, `face_front`,
    `face_side`, `custom` (needs `--cam`: camera position, look-at target and
    vertical span in metres, square 800px image).
  - `--parts body,head,boots` renders only those cached parts (much faster);
    without `--parts` everything is rendered.
  - preview.sh writes `<OUTDIR>/<view>.png` and `<OUTDIR>/sheet.png`.
  - A full render takes 1-3 minutes on this 4-core machine. Other agents
    render at the same time, so prefer `--parts` and `--res 0.6`-`0.8`.
* Overlay on the reference: `$BP compare.py <render ref.png> <out.png>`
  (reference | render | red silhouette over reference).
* Use the Read tool to look at PNGs. Crop/zoom with PIL into your own
  scratch folder.

Coordinates: metres, Blender Z up, the character faces -Y (towards the
camera), her RIGHT side is -X (viewer's left). Height 1.68 m.
`rig.px(x_px, y_px, depth)` converts a pixel of the 735x1030 reference
image to model space (1.85 mm per pixel) at the given depth (Y). Joint
positions are in `rig.J` (shoulder_R, elbow_R, wrist_R, hand_R, hip_L,
knee_L, ankle_L, toe_L, neck_base, neck_top, ...).

### Useful modules

* `hardsurf.py` (NEW, use it for anything rigid): `prism(poly2d, depth, origin, R)`,
  `frame_prism(outer, inner, depth, ...)` (buckle frames), `box`, `lathe(profile, seg, origin, R)`
  (rings, cylinders, caps), `cylinder`, `sweep(profile2d, path, up)` (straps,
  bands following a curve), 2D profiles `rounded_rect`, `chamfer_rect`, `circle`,
  `frame_from(x=..., z=...)`, `place`, `merge_vf`, and
  `hard_mesh(items, mat, bevel=0.0008, segments=2, angle=30)` which returns a
  MeshData flagged `hard`: in Blender it gets smooth shading + sharp edges by
  angle + a Bevel modifier with hardened normals. Faces may be n-gons.
  Closed meshes get their normals recalculated automatically.
* `meshutil.py`: `MeshData(verts, faces, mat=..., uv=None, subsurf=0,
  outline=True, outline_w=1.0, decimate=0, props={})`, `merge`, `catmull`
  (spline resample), `loft_tube`, `grid_surface`, `polygon_sd`, `clip_mesh`
  (cut a triangle mesh exactly along the zero level of a scalar field),
  `clean_mesh`.
  props understood by scene.py: `hard`, `bevel`, `bevel_seg`, `sharp_angle`,
  `recalc` (recalculate normals), `solidify` (thickness of an open sheet,
  outward), `solid_offset`, `shell_mat` (second material for the outer/inner
  side), `normals: 'smooth'` (diffused custom normals for organic MC meshes).
  `decimate=N` = target face count (only for big marching-cubes meshes).
* `sdf.py`: SDF primitives (`Sphere, Ellipsoid, RoundCone, Box, Torus,
  Cylinder, Loft`), `Grid` (sample, `union/subtract/map`, `.mesh(level)`),
  `gradient(fn, P)`. Use SDF only for organic shapes.
* `body.py`: body shapes: `B.leg('L')`, `B.arm('R')`, `B.neck()`,
  `B.torso()`, `B.BodySDF()` (whole body, callable on (N,3) points),
  limb lofts have `.origin`, `.R`, `.prof(name, z)`.
* `outfit.py` helpers: `surface_point(fn, x, z, offset=...)` (first point
  from the front where fn < offset), `body_fn`, `limb_local(loft, P)`
  (x, y, s-down-the-limb), `piece(g, inner, outer, mask, mat)` (garment
  layer = offset surface of a field clipped by a mask, thickness by
  Solidify), `front_poly(poly_px, ycut)` (mask from a polygon traced in
  reference pixels), `zrange`, `slab`, `inter`, `union`, `minus`,
  `foot_loft(side, grow)`.
* Materials (names in `materials.PALETTE`): skin, face, hair, hair_dark,
  coat, coat_trim, lining, red, red_dark, black, leather, metal, chrome,
  gunmetal, rubber, brass, white, decal_white, sole, sheer, glow_blue,
  glow_red, glow_pink, lens, gold, gray, blade. If you truly need a new
  one, create/extend `src/materials_extra.py` with
  `EXTRA = {'<yourpart>_<name>': ('#lit', '#shadow', '#outline', dict(thr=0.4))}`
  (prefix names with your part; never remove other entries).

## Rules

1. Edit ONLY the files your task names (plus new helper files you create,
   prefixed with your part name, and `materials_extra.py` entries prefixed
   with your part). Do not edit scene.py, materials.py, parts.py, body.py,
   rig.py, hair.py or other parts' modules. If something outside your files
   blocks you, say so in your report instead of editing it.
2. Do not run git commands. Do not delete cache files of other parts.
3. Keep object names stable and descriptive with your part's prefix (the
   rig assigns objects to bones by name; list every object you create in
   your report).
4. Build with `$BP build_parts.py <part>` and confirm in renders. Compare
   against the reference with the `ref` view and `compare.py` (silhouette
   must stay aligned with the reference), plus close-ups with `--cam`.
5. Keep the triangle budget reasonable (state your part's count).
6. Save your final verification renders into the folder given in your task.

## Quality bar (what "not clay" means here)

* Rigid things (buckles, rings, soles, plates, swords, goggles, clips) are
  polygon meshes with real flat faces and 0.3-1 mm bevels, not blobs.
* Cloth has thickness, hard rims at hems, and designed shapes (folds,
  seams, layered panels) where the reference shows them.
* Details from the references are present and in the right place, with
  the right proportions and colours.
* Shapes read clearly in silhouette from front, side and back.
